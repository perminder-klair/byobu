Byōbu is a wall display OS for a Raspberry Pi with a touchscreen: faces for the room, a home screen for your hand, in the look of Omarchy. Everything it shows comes from plugins. Home Assistant is one, and it works without it.

It's Arch Linux ARM with [cage](https://github.com/cage-kiosk/cage) running Chromium in kiosk mode. Omarchy's desktop stack doesn't run here, but its themes do.

A byōbu is a Japanese folding screen: painted panels, hinged together, that stand in a room. This one hangs on a wall.

## What you need

- A Raspberry Pi 4
- The 7" Touch Display 2, rotated to landscape
- An SD card of more than 6 GB, as the rest of the card becomes a data partition
- Wi-Fi or Ethernet
- An Arch Linux machine to build the image on

A speaker on the 3.5 mm jack or USB gives you sound and timers you can hear, and a USB microphone lets you talk to the [assistant](/manual/assistant/). Neither is required.

## How far along it is

<Note title="where it's up to">

Byōbu is early. There's no download yet, so you [build the image yourself](/manual/getting-started/), and it has only run on one panel so far. AirPlay, Spotify, voice, the assistant and pairing a phone have been tried with stand-ins rather than the real things, and recent changes to the image builder have only been tried on a running panel, not a freshly flashed one.

</Note>
